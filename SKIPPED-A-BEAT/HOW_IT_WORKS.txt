HOW IT WORKS - Skipped a Beat
JIN2 · @jin_22ee

The body was drawn in letters from a picture that isn't mine,
so the picture is not included. Here is how the 480 frames were made,
and what skipped_a_beat.py does with them.

How the frames were made
1. Two layers
   The picture was split into the blue body and the red heart, by how
   much more red than blue each pixel has.

2. The flinch
   Every 1.6 seconds the body gets a hard kick: in 0.05 seconds it grows
   by 6 percent and the shoulders jump up, then it shivers back down.
   A smaller second beat follows 0.3 seconds later, like a heartbeat.
   The screen shakes a few pixels while it happens.

3. The heart
   Around the heart the picture is pushed outwards, strongest in the
   middle, so the heart swells out of the chest on every flinch.
   A white spark in the heart flares up at the same moment.

4. The shock wave
   After each flinch a ring of light runs out from the heart along the
   veins and fades on its way.

5. From pixels to letters
   Each frame was cut into a grid of 120 x 68 letter cells:
   - brightness picks the letter, from -:;= (dark) to B8$#@ (bright),
   - the letters flicker a little, like code that is running,
   - the colour is deep blue, blue or pale blue for the body,
     red or pink for the heart, white for the spark.
   In the first second and a half the letters arrive from the heart outwards.

What skipped_a_beat.py does
6. Plays the frames
   For each frame it draws the letters in their colour, lets the last
   frame fade behind it and adds a soft glow. On every flinch the red
   slips right and the blue slips left for a moment.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   TRAIL             how long the last frame stays behind
   SPLIT             how far the colours pull apart
   CW, CH, FONT_PX   letter size
