HOW IT WORKS - Swan
JIN2 · @jin_22ee

The artwork is one Python file plus a small data file. Two swans glide
on dark water: one comes in from the right, the other from far back on
the left. They meet, lower their heads until the beaks touch, and their
necks make a heart. Then the second swan slips behind the first, turns
far back, and follows it out of frame. Every part of it is made of
letters.

1. The shape
   The swan is a cloud of about 30,000 points stored in swan_shape.npz:
   a side view traced as a flat outline, then puffed into a rounded
   body (the distance from the outline sets the depth, and a mirrored
   copy makes the far side). Each point keeps a surface normal and a
   brightness, so the body is lit like a solid and reads as one when it
   turns.

2. The neck
   A centre line runs from the breast, up the neck, over the crown and
   down to the beak tip. It is split into bones, and each neck point
   remembers which bone it belongs to and where it sits beside it. Each
   frame the bones are rotated a little (stretch, dip, sway) and the
   points follow, so the neck bends smoothly while the head stays one
   rigid piece. A weight blends neck points into the body so there is
   no seam.

3. The motion
   Each swan follows a path of (time, x, z) waypoints with a smooth
   monotone curve between them. The heading comes from the direction of
   travel, so the body turns with the path and is seen from the side,
   the back or the front as it goes. Small slow waves on top (neck,
   head turn, wing lift, body sway, bob) keep it alive.

4. The water
   A grid of points on the water plane carries three overlapping ripple
   waves, a brighter patch around each swan, a V wake behind it, rings
   pushed out by the breast every few seconds, and one ring where the
   beaks meet. The swan's reflection is its own points flipped below the
   waterline and shifted by the ripples. Sun flecks twinkle on the
   surface.

5. Letters
   Every point is projected onto a grid of letters. Far points are drawn
   first and near ones paint over them. Brighter cells get denser
   letters (from 1 i l up to 8 B $ #), the outline gets / | \ - _ in the
   direction it runs, and the water uses ~ - _ by brightness.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   SWANS       the paths and motion curves of the two swans
   STRAIGHT, DIP   how the neck bends
   TAG_COLOR   the colours
   CELL        letter size
   LOOP        length of the loop
