HOW IT WORKS - Flip Phone
JIN2 · @jin_22ee

1. The phone
   Each part is a flat sheet of points: the two shells, the screen,
   twelve keys, two soft keys, the round d-pad, the speaker, the hinge
   and the antenna. The screen half keeps its own frame so it can swing.

2. The ring
   From 3.2 seconds it buzzes for 1.2 seconds and rests for 0.8.
   While it buzzes the whole phone shivers a few millimetres and turns
   a hair from side to side. A small light on the lid blinks while it is shut.

3. Opening
   At 5 seconds the screen half swings up around the hinge over 1.4 seconds.
   It goes a little past open and settles back, like a real lid.

4. The screen
   It flickers on two or three times and turns green.
   The handset and the name are drawn once on a small picture; every frame
   that picture is laid onto the screen with a homography (the map between
   the four screen corners and the picture), and the letter cells that land
   on the dark parts are left empty. The name scrolls across, like on old phones.

5. Light
   A band of light drifts across the glass, the rim is a little brighter,
   and tiny sparks come and go. The green light falls on the keys near
   the hinge and fades with distance. Around the screen a soft green glow
   swells with every buzz, and specks of green dust float up in front of it.

6. From points to letters
   Every point is projected into a grid of 216 x 137 letter cells:
   - where one part ends, the outline uses / | \ - _ in the direction of the edge,
   - inside, brightness picks the letter, from 1il!;: (dark) to 08B$#&%Q (bright),
   - the letters flicker a little, like code that is running.
   In the first three seconds the letters gather from the bottom up.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   CALLER            the name on the screen
   RING, OPEN        when it rings and when it opens
   TRAIL             how long the last frame stays behind
   CW, CH, FONT_PX   letter size
