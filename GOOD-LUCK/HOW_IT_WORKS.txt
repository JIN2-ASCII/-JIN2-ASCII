HOW IT WORKS - Good Luck
JIN2 · @jin_22ee

The whole artwork is one Python file. A four-leaf clover is covered in
rows of "Good luck" in 77 languages, and the letters alone make its shape.

1. The phrases
   Each phrase was drawn once as a picture, because scripts like Arabic,
   Hindi or Thai need their letters joined and shaped correctly.
   Right-to-left languages are typed in from the right.

2. The shape
   Each leaf is one heart: half a heart as a list of 48 points, mirrored,
   with its tip in the middle of the clover. Four of them point out in
   four directions, and a curved stem runs down from the middle.

3. Rows of text
   The clover is cut into rows. In each row, phrases are picked so they
   fill the row from edge to edge, like justified text, and leftover
   room gets one more, slightly smaller phrase. English and Korean come
   up every fourth phrase. The front and the back get different phrases.

4. Depth
   Every leaf gets its own thickness: flat sides, a face that bulges
   towards its middle and a slightly raised rim. The stem is a thinner
   rod. The sides carry short phrases too.

5. Turning and glass
   The clover turns to the right around its middle, once every
   10 seconds. Each row is drawn in half-pixel steps, far to near, so
   the turned leaves have no holes. The colour is green glass: pale in
   the middle, deep green at the thick edge, white where the surface
   faces the light.

6. Light and sparkles
   Every 4 seconds a band of light runs across the glass, and small
   green stars rise around the clover and twinkle.

7. The start
   In the first 3 seconds the phrases are typed in one by one.
   Each frame fades into the next and a soft glow is added.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   LEAF, LEAF_W      size and width of the leaves
   ANGLES            which way the leaves point
   WALL, DOME        how thick and how puffy it is
   TURN              seconds for one turn
   PALE, DEEP        the glass colours
